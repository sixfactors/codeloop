codeloop, designed as a workplace for people and agents

The target design for codeloop's interface, built from the bottom up: the things a team tracks, the fields on each, the small pieces that show them, and the screens those pieces add up to. It covers the Jira-style project management and the wiki as one product. It describes how it should be, not how it is today.

Reading order: the job, the objects, the fields and states, the building blocks, the screens, the main flows, and what is different from Jira. The build order and the decisions needed from you are at the end.

1The job it does

A founder or a small team describes work. Agents do most of it. People decide at a few points and want proof before they do. The interface exists for three moments of the day:

"What needs me?"

Approvals, questions an agent asked, work that is failing or stuck. Answer each in place, with the evidence beside it, in minutes.

"What is moving?"

Which stories are in flight, who holds each one (an agent or a person), what stage it is at, and whether its checks pass.

"What next, and what shipped?"

Rank the plan, fill Up next, see releases and what each delivered. Read why a decision was made, in the wiki.

Two principles follow from this. Nothing is approved without its proof on the same screen. An agent is a teammate with a name and a status, shown the same way a person is, so "who has this" always has an answer.

States: what people see, and what agents do inside them

Today's lanes and nine stages are too many to read. People need six states. The stage inside "In progress" is shown as a sub-label, not a column, unless a team turns it on.

Backlog → Up next → In progress · Shape / Spec / Build / Test → Needs you → Done → Shipped in v0.5.0

Two flags can sit on any state: Failing when the last check failed, and Blocked when a linked story is not done. "Needs you" is not a stage the work moves into; it is shown whenever a gate, a question or a failure waits on a person, so the board never hides it.

The wiki is a Notion-style reader and editor with typed pages. Every page shows who owns it, when it was last checked against the code, and which stories changed it. A gotcha seen three times becomes critical and appears in the brief of any story that touches its area.

Everywhere: command palette, peek, and keyboard

⌘K finds any story, page or person, and runs any action: approve, answer, create, move. Peek opens a story in a side sheet over any list, so triage never loses its place. j / k move, a approves, r sends back, c creates. Every list supports multi-select and bulk change.

6The four flows that matter most

Ask becomes a story

Type in Claude, or "Describe a problem" in Plan. An agent writes the story with acceptance lines and puts it in Backlog for you to rank. Nothing starts until it reaches Up next.

Approve with proof

Home shows the gate. Open proof shows each acceptance line, its test and its last run, the review verdict and the commits. Approve records what you saw.

Answer an agent

Each question has a recommended answer. Accept with one key, or write your own. The agent continues on its own.

Find out why

From a story, the Wiki tab lists the pages it read and changed. From a page, "changed by" lists the stories and their decisions.

9Build order, bottom up

  1. Data: the story's fields above, links, holder versus owner, states derived from stages. One API call per screen returns everything that screen needs.
  2. Building blocks: the twelve pieces in section 4, as shared components with a page that shows each in every state.
  3. Patterns: row, tile, peek, record, doc.
  4. Screens: Story record first, because every other screen links to it. Then Home, Board, Plan, Wiki, Releases, Reports.
  5. Everywhere: command palette, keyboard, bulk change, saved views.

Each step ships behind a browser test and an independent design review before you see it.
